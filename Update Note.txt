[i]Everything released since 31 July 2026.[/i]

Most of this update is about the desktop pet. It can now be dressed, looked after and played with, it remembers who it is between sessions, and six new characters come with it. Alongside that, scenes gained a visual editor with animation and 3D, FrontEngine can now publish to the Steam Workshop as well as import from it, and a long list of smaller tools arrived.

[hr][/hr]

[h2]Six characters and a wardrobe[/h2]

FrontEngine now comes with six drawn characters, [b]MOCHI[/b], [b]MOSS[/b], [b]PIP[/b], [b]BOLT[/b], [b]INK[/b] and [b]GILL[/b], in a three-ink style of black, vermilion and off-white. With them come fourteen outfits made of thirty pieces, each drawn to fit every one of the six. On the Pet page, pick a character, press [b]Use built-in character[/b] and then [b]Spawn pet[/b]: your pet appears as that character, ready to be dressed in the wardrobe.

The wardrobe works by slot: head, neck, body and back. Putting on a piece replaces whatever already sits in that slot and tells you what it took off. Each character also has eye, mouth and body-part choices and colour regions you can repaint, and you can pin the current look beside your changes to compare before you apply them. Each pet can keep up to twenty saved outfits, and a pet that is busy with something changes clothes once it is done.

Undressed and in its default look, a character walks and climbs with hand-drawn step frames. Dressed, it uses the layered animation that keeps every piece in place.

[h2]A pet you can look after[/h2]

A pet now has a mind of its own. By default it picks what to do next at random from everything it can do: walk, sit, play, groom, climb the nearer wall, wave, nap, celebrate or show off its outfit. It sets off walking the moment you spawn it. For a quieter pet that only walks and idles, choose Walk on floor instead of Cycle all actions under Behaviour.

[list]
[*][b]Stroke it[/b] with the mouse and it reacts.
[*][b]Treats[/b] come from a menu, and each pet remembers which ones it likes.
[*][b]Toys[/b]: throw a ball for it to fetch, even from the top of a window, or shine a light spot for it to chase.
[*][b]Come here[/b] calls it to a place you choose, with the mouse or the keyboard.
[*][b]Photos[/b] save a picture of your pet with a transparent background.
[*][b]Greetings[/b]: it welcomes you back and says goodbye, and the way it treats you grows through relationship stages over time.
[*][b]Focus timer[/b]: while the timer runs, the pet switches between play, work and rest, and keeps out of the way while you work. It also settles down while you are typing.
[/list]

Each pet is now its own saved identity, with a name, mood, fullness and affection. Choose one before spawning to pick up where it left off, start a new one, or clone a pet together with its appearance. How often a pet talks and how large its animations are can be set for each pet, and every pet command can be reached from the keyboard in the pet manager. A pet pack editor lets you map your own walk, idle, sleep, climb, fall and drag images and export them as a pack.

Pets can also be Imervue Puppet models, with their own reactions, petting, toys and photos; this needs the Imervue runtime. The six built-in characters can be exported as rigged Puppet models with Use built-in Puppet model. Puppet pets stroll on their own too, and now and then sit, play, groom, wave or celebrate in place.

[b]Fixed:[/b] a walking pet's animation could freeze while it moved, so it seemed to slide across the screen. The frames now change as it walks. A pet standing on a window narrower than itself no longer shakes left and right. With monitors at different scaling, a pet moved to another monitor keeps its size and stays on that monitor.

[h2]Scenes: a visual editor, animation and 3D[/h2]

Scenes can now be built in a visual editor with undo, instead of by hand. A timeline animates each layer's position and opacity with keyframes, has fade-in and slide-in starters, and switching scenes crossfades. Three starter templates, Work desktop, Teaching and Focus, give you a layout to edit instead of an empty page. Video, web and Puppet layers can be previewed live in the editor, muted until you turn them on.

Choose 3D in the same editor to place GLB, glTF and OBJ models, planes, cubes, spheres, cameras and lights, move them with handles, and play model animations on the scene's shared clock.

A scene can also be sent on its own to a virtual camera at a fixed 640x480, 1280x720 or 1920x1080, whatever is on your desktop. Rules (below) can load, play and stop scenes and change their layers.

[h2]Publish to the Steam Workshop[/h2]

Presets, then Manage Workshop, now publishes as well as imports. Scenes, preset packages and sprite pet packs can be shared, privately too. Subscribed items are checked before they are used, and an interrupted upload can be picked up again.

[h2]New on screen and on the Tools page[/h2]

[list]
[*][b]Screensaver[/b]: after a set idle time, show the video, image, GIF, particles or web page you chose, and take it down when you come back.
[*][b]Window replica[/b]: an always-on-top live copy of another window, or of just part of it.
[*][b]Span mode[/b]: one overlay stretched across every screen.
[*][b]Freeze[/b]: hold the current frame of a monitor while you get the next thing ready behind it. Ctrl+Shift+F7 releases it.
[*][b]Pinned captures[/b] float on top, and a [b]reference board[/b] holds several pictures on one canvas.
[*][b]Capture editor[/b]: crop and annotate a capture before you use it.
[*][b]Record area[/b] now writes GIF or silent AVI, with pause and resume.
[*][b]Live OCR window[/b]: keep the text of a screen region pinned beside it, refreshed when you ask or every few seconds.
[*][b]Compare images[/b]: side by side, overlaid, with a sliding wipe, or as a colour difference.
[*][b]Colour palette[/b]: collect picked colours into named, grouped swatches.
[*][b]Whiteboard[/b] pages, with selecting, moving and deleting strokes, and undo across pages.
[/list]

[h2]Automation and organising[/h2]

[list]
[*][b]Rules[/b]: "when these conditions hold, do this." Combine a weekday, a time window and the focused app, then apply a preset, show, hide or close the overlays, set the quality, or drive a scene. Rules have priorities, cooldowns and a history of what ran.
[*][b]Preset schedule[/b]: apply a preset on chosen days at a set time.
[*][b]Preset versions[/b]: each preset keeps its last fifty versions to compare and restore.
[*][b]Keep awake[/b] stops the display sleeping while overlays are up.
[*][b]Media keys[/b], overlays that stay on one virtual desktop, and moving a window to another monitor.
[*][b]Follow a window[/b]: an overlay that stays attached to another program's window as it moves.
[*][b]Monitor profiles[/b]: save and restore where windows sit for each combination of monitors.
[*][b]Command search[/b]: Ctrl+K finds and runs any command, in your language.
[*][b]Tasks and Today[/b]: a task list with due times, calendar import from .ics files, and a Today widget.
[*][b]Capture history[/b], searchable by the text in your captures and recognised on your own machine, and [b]image clipboard history[/b]. Both are off until you turn them on.
[*][b]Asset library[/b]: tag, search and favourite the images, pet packs and scenes you use.
[/list]

[h2]A tidier interface[/h2]

Pages now put the action you use most first and keep the rest in menus. The Control Center leads with four direct buttons, advanced tool sections open only when you need them, and settings rows stack neatly when the window is narrow. The shortcut list shows the keys that are actually bound.
