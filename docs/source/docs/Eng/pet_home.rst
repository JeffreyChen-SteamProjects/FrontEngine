Pet home Page
-------------

**Pet home** — the first page. Your saved pets move into a small home, live there on their own and work for home coins.

* *Create a home* gives a bed, a dining table, a writing desk and 10 coins. Nothing is created or started until you press it, and every other tool works without a home.
* *Move in* a saved pet or make a *New pet*. A pet lives either in the home or on the desktop, never both at once; its energy, fullness, mood and affection carry over and go back to its profile when it moves out.
* *Arrange* puts furniture from storage on the floor. A red preview says why a spot does not work (outside the home, something already there, blocks the entrance); Esc stops arranging. *Put away* returns a piece to storage, and work at it pauses with its progress kept.
* *Work* needs no setting up: during their working hours pets take up whatever their workstations allow, their own trade first, using only materials already in the backpack, and the tab lists what each is doing with a picture of what it makes; any job can be called off. Each finished unit pays exactly once, and a cancelled one pays nothing. The starter furniture is set out by itself, *Place* puts a stored piece in the first free spot (or *Arrange* and click the floor), furniture bought in the *Shop* goes straight into the home, and finished *Getting started* rewards and the commissions you have the goods for settle themselves. The shop, backpack and storage show every piece and item as a picture. The *Settings* tab keeps only showing the home on the desktop and reducing motion.
* *Shop* sells extra furniture; selling returns at most half the price, and the basic bed, table and desk are never sold.
* The page is laid out like a game screen: the home on top with its day, time, weather, coins and *Start* / *Pause* on one line, and *Residents*, *Work*, *Furniture*, *Shop*, *Backpack*, *Goals*, *Inbox* and *Settings* as tabs underneath, so the home stays in view while you use them. Before there is a home, the top shows the bed, table and desk a new home starts with beside *Create a home*. Under the overview sit *Back to the desktop*, *Backpack*, *Goals* and the zoom (− / + or Ctrl+wheel), and a *Waiting for you* list of what the home cannot settle by itself (a pet to move in, unread notes, furniture left in storage, jobs waiting for materials, a ready celebration, a wish); clicking a line opens its tab. While arranging, the desktop strip shows *Done arranging*, since it never takes the keyboard. A save from a newer FrontEngine offers *Get the latest FrontEngine*. *Free arranging* switches to a separate home (``pet-home-sandbox.json``) with every piece of furniture, no costs and no coins; nothing done there reaches the managed home, its coins and goals, or the pets' own values, and switching back or forth cancels what the pets were doing without rewards and saves first.
* Time passes only while the home is running (*Start* / *Pause*); time while FrontEngine was closed or the computer slept is never simulated. The home is saved to ``pet-home.json`` beside your settings, with the previous good copy kept as ``pet-home.json.bak``. A save that cannot be read, or one from a newer version, is never overwritten: the page offers the backup, or keeps the old file and starts a new home.
* Pets dressed as a layered character (a built-in INKSIDE character or one from the wardrobe) appear in the home as themselves: walking while on the way, sitting at the desk or in the armchair, sleeping in bed, eating at the table, playing at the toy box, greeting each other, facing the way they walk. *Wardrobe…* on a resident opens the usual wardrobe, and a new outfit shows up in the home at once. Characters are loaded in the background; until then a coloured marker stands in. Pets without a layered character (picture pets, new pets) appear in the home as one of the built-in characters, the same one every time; their own look and their desktop pet are not changed. An undressed built-in character with its default parts and colours has its own drawn poses: it lies on the bed to sleep, sits in the armchair, eats, plays, writes at the desk, waters the planter and hammers at the workbench (sitting, sleeping, eating and playing also on the desktop). Dressed or recoloured, it keeps the layered animation and sits at its station. A pet still wearing an older version of a built-in character is moved to the current one automatically, keeping its outfit and colours.
* Four home *modes*: *Normal*; *Focus*, which keeps the desktop strip quiet (no bubbles or cards); *Meeting*, which hides the home and pauses it and says so; and *Power saver*, which draws the strip at 15 frames per second without slowing the pets' lives. Sharing your screen switches to Meeting for as long as it lasts. The *Inbox* collects finished work, needs, life moments and problems, folds repeats into one line and never pops up or takes focus; pets eating and napping on their own are never listed.
* The home has days, nights and weather: the overview's sky follows the game clock from dawn to night, and clear, cloudy or rainy spells change every six game hours, chosen from the home itself so they are the same each time. Weather is atmosphere only; it never damages anything or makes you wait. Three themes (*Warm home*, *Green studio*, *Night studio*) change colours, never the layout. Furniture is drawn as outlined cartoon pictures modelled in Blender, coloured to match each theme; a piece's name shows while it is selected or while arranging. Rain, starry nights, a first close friend, getting good at a job, a green home, a full house, a first short story and a good harvest join the life moments, twelve in all. *Reduce motion* stops pets gliding between steps and keeps the rain still; every event is also written in the card and the moments list, and the home makes no sound.
* *Goals* grow with the home: a home level that rises with milestones you reach by living (all three jobs, more residents, more furniture, close friendships), ten *Getting started* goals with small rewards collected by themselves (skip them any time; they stay reviewable), three *commissions* a day that buy goods for coins with no streaks or missed-day penalties, a small wish per pet that says how to fulfil it and never counts down, life moments such as birthdays, a first handmade creation or dinner with friends, and a one-time home celebration once two or more pets live together and all three jobs have been done. *Take a photo* saves the drawing of the home to ``pet-album`` beside your settings; nothing from your desktop is captured. The backpack shows how much of the furniture collection you own.
* Pets need comfort and company too. Beds, armchairs and decorations make a home comfortable; low comfort wears on mood. Pets say hello to each other, enjoy meals and games together and grow closer, and now and then squabble over the last free seat. Each pet has a lasting personality (diligent, playful, quiet, sociable, or a liking for gardening, writing or crafting) that shapes what it chooses and makes liked work a little faster; no personality makes a pet unfit for anything. The card shows personality, closest friends and how many minutes the current activity has left, and on the desktop short bubbles say what a pet just started, who it greeted and what it earned. Older home saves gain the new needs on load; a home saved by this version is protected from older FrontEngine versions.
* Three jobs: writing at the desk, gardening at a *Planter* (herbs that make meals happier, flowers to sell) and crafting at a *Workbench* with wood from the shop (toys to sell, stools that go straight into storage). Work runs in visible phases (sow, water, harvest…) and waits rather than starting when materials are missing. The *Backpack* lists everything the home owns, what is set aside for work, and sells goods. Buying always costs more than selling returns, and no chain of jobs can turn something back into itself.
* The home also lives on the desktop: a transparent strip along the bottom of the screen, standing on the taskbar edge. Only pets, furniture and the small ⌂ button take the mouse; every other pixel clicks through to the windows behind, and the strip never takes focus. Click a pet for its card, right-click it for commands, drag it to move it; right-click furniture to put it away. *Show the home on the desktop* turns it off, *Desktop layer* puts it above or below other windows (above by default), Hide all pauses the home until it is shown again, and the Control Center's quality setting picks 60, 30 or 15 frames per second without changing how fast the home lives.
